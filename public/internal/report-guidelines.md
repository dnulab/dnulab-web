# DNU Lab Research Report Guidelines

For most DNU Lab research projects, we recommend culminating the project with a high-quality research report. The learning goal of the report is to achieve a quality of writing and presentation at the level of a peer-reviewed conference or journal paper. The depth, breadth, impact, and novelty of the results reported are not important. It is preferable to write a short report of high quality rather than a longer report of lower quality.

Typically, we recommend choosing just one result or experiment to include in the report. The report should prioritize a coherent, polished description and analysis over a complete project history.

Reports should adhere to requirements for submission to the main track of the Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS) conference in the current calendar year, except where otherwise noted. DNU Lab reports are modeled on NeurIPS-style technical writing and formatting, but they are not expected to meet the novelty, significance, or impact standard of a NeurIPS submission.

At the time of writing, the calendar year is 2026, meaning that the requirements are taken from the 2026 NeurIPS main track handbook: [https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook)

The key requirements are listed in the Author section under the subsection titled “Paper Formatting Instructions.” DNU Lab reports do not need to include the NeurIPS paper checklist, but they should adhere to all other requirements listed there. The most important requirement is that the report may contain a maximum of *nine* content pages, accompanied by additional pages containing references and optional appendices.

Another important section of the author guidelines is “Author Use of Agents and Large Language Models (LLMs).” All requirements listed here apply to DNU Lab reports. The key idea is that “while authors are welcome to use any tool they wish for preparing and writing the paper, they must ensure that all content is correct and original.” Also note that, as described below, all DNU Lab reports must have a separate section describing the use of AI assistance in creating the report.

A DNU Lab report does not require the other aspects of a NeurIPS submission, such as verified conformance to ethics guidelines. However, we do recommend you read through the NeurIPS requirements to understand the expectations for peer-reviewed research in artificial intelligence.

You are encouraged to use LaTeX for typesetting the report, including the NeurIPS template. However, this is not required. Any document preparation system is permitted, as long as the report meets the formatting requirements. The report should be submitted as a PDF file.

## Structure of the report

The following sections are required: Title, Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, Conclusion, References, and Use Of Artificial Intelligence. The following sections are recommended or optional, depending on the project: Background, Experimental Design, Results, Analysis, Discussion, Appendices, and any other sections suitable for the content.

Guidance for individual sections is given below. Remember that concise reports are preferred, so suggested section lengths can be reduced. Please don't expand material beyond what is necessary for clarity and completeness. It is not important to fill the nine-page content limit. 

**Title:** Aim for 8 to 15 words. Try to describe your contribution accurately.

**Abstract:** Aim for 150 to 200 words. Summarize the report clearly, using language that can be understood without reading the whole report.

**Introduction:** It is probably best to write this last. The length can vary depending on the nature of the project. At a minimum, explain the same content as the abstract but in more detail. Add more context for the motivation of your report, and preview some main results. In many reports, the introduction will be about one page, but it may be shorter or longer depending on the project.

**Related Work:** DNU Lab reports are not expected to include a comprehensive literature survey. However, it is essential to include a Related Work section. This section must discuss at least one item of related work. It should explain how the contribution of the current report differs from, complements, or reproduces the results of related work. In other words, the section should not merely summarize related work; it should explain the relationship between that work and the current report. In many reports, this section will between a quarter page and one page. You are welcome to include the following text verbatim without citation, or adapt your own version of it:

```
This report describes the output of an independent research project in the DNU Lab of Dickinson College. By design, DNU Lab projects focus on conducting research rather than surveying existing work. In this section, we describe only one[/two/three...] piece(s) of related work. This serves as an initial step toward a more complete literature survey.
```

The subsequent sections are more content-dependent. You may have sections with titles similar to some of the following: Background, Experimental Design, Results, Analysis, Discussion. These sections should be chosen to support the main contribution of the report, rather than to record every step taken during the project.

Figures and tables should be used when they help explain the project, support the main argument, or summarize important results. Every figure and table should be readable, have a clear caption, and be referenced from the main text.

**Conclusion:** Summarize the contribution and mention any future work. In many reports, the conclusion will be one or two paragraphs.

**References:** This is a required section. The bibliography may use any standard style. It is not required to follow NeurIPS style. Every work in the bibliography should be cited at least once from the text. The references section does not count toward the nine-page content limit.

**Use of Artificial Intelligence:** This is a required section. Initially aim for 100 to 200 words, but make this section longer if needed to provide an accurate explanation. As with the references and appendices, this section does not count toward your nine-page content limit. The section should describe the main ways in which AI tools were used to guide research, develop theory, perform experiments, write code, analyze results, write the report, and for any other significant purpose. It should also explain what the human contributions were.

**Appendices:** These are optional. They can include additional experiments, results, discussion, details of implementation, and so on. The main report should be understandable without reading the appendices. Appendices should provide supplemental material rather than essential material needed to understand the central contribution.

## Checklist

Before submitting the report, check that it satisfies the nine-page content limit; includes references, a related work section, and a use-of-AI section; captions and references all figures and tables; uses clear and consistent formatting; and has been checked for spelling, grammar, punctuation, and capitalization errors.
